What is learned—and what is enforced?

Graph policy

Six features per bank describe balances, queued inflows/outflows, counts and observed outage status. Normalized graph messages feed a trained 6 × 12 matrix and tanh layer. An edge head ranks candidate payments using source/destination embeddings and five payment features.

The model distils a two-step lookahead teacher using softmax cross-entropy. Stress training includes normal, outage, delay and burst scenarios. The edge-only ablation removes graph messages.

Feasibility gate

Only arrived, unsettled payments from an available sender with sufficient cash may be selected. Transfers conserve integer cash and cannot create credit. The model chooses among feasible payments; it cannot override the gate.

Cycle netting searches feasible directed cycles of 2–4 whole payments and applies their net balance changes simultaneously. It has a different settlement capability and is labelled separately.

Limits, originality and reproducibility

This is an exploratory synthetic benchmark, not a bank deployment or evidence of a new state-of-the-art algorithm. The proposed project contribution is the comparison of stress training, graph messages and settlement mechanisms under controlled failures.

Real liquidity-saving mechanisms and ML payment-system research already exist. See Bank of Canada research and the ECB/Bank of Japan Stella report.

Assumptions: one currency; 100 payments; 60 ticks; no fees, collateral, credit, central-bank injections, settlement capacity limit or interbank borrowing. Outage duration is only used by the simulator; policy features see its current status. The teacher sees queued payments, never future arrivals. Synthetic results do not establish real-world performance.
